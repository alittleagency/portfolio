Cox Automotive: One Trusted Data Layer for AI at Scale

An outside-in look by Brittany May, AI Solutions Architect

02 / 07

What I see

  • Cox Automotive is an Atlanta-based, roughly $10B services and software company spanning Autotrader, KBB, Manheim, Dealertrack and more (verify).
  • The AI-first push is public: 100 of 680 scrum teams on agentic development, all teams targeted by end of 2026 (verify).
  • Cox Automotive Intelligence launched at NADA 2026, covering pricing, inventory, financing and engagement (verify).
  • Fullpath, an AI customer data platform, closed June 2026. Cox's own line: "Data is your moat" (verify).
03 / 07

The opportunity

  • From the outside, it looks like data fragmentation across a dozen-plus brands slows every AI product that needs one view of a dealer, consumer or vehicle.
  • From the outside, it looks like agent governance is still being defined. Without shared evaluation gates, drift risks bad outputs and lost trust (verify).
  • From the outside, it looks like many dealers lack clean data, so customer-facing AI may stall at adoption, not at the model.
04 / 07

What others are doing

  • OPENLANE unified its AI and data tools as OPENLANE Intelligence, with AI damage detection and condition-report analysis (verify).
  • CarGurus has launched AI tools for search and dealership visits, focused on the consumer marketplace (verify date).
  • Their footprints are narrower, so unifying data is easier for them.
  • Cox could stand apart by turning its breadth into one governed data layer that no competitor can copy.
05 / 07

My 30 / 60 / 90 day plan

  • 30 days: Map the data and AI landscape across brands, interview product and IT leads, and name the top three identity and data bottlenecks.
  • 60 days: Publish a reference architecture and an entity resolution pilot linking dealer, consumer and vehicle records across two brands.
  • 90 days: Launch an evaluation standard for agentic products, with realistic test tasks, rubrics, accept/revise/reject gates and audit trails.
06 / 07

Why me

  • At the University of Michigan I built MDM from scratch, with entity resolution and deduplication, across 50M+ records a year.
  • At an SBA lender I connected sales, document and compliance systems, cutting manual entry 40% and speeding approvals 35%.
  • I write expert tasks and rubrics and grade model output for a top AI lab, so I see where models fail on real work.
  • I built three production-ready apps with Claude as development partner. I am an architect and builder, not a software engineer.
07 / 07

Where this could go

  • Head of AI Solutions: own the cross-brand reference architecture so every AI product draws on one trusted view of the data.
  • Set the evaluation and guardrail standard that lets teams ship agents with confidence, and bridge product, IT and dealer-facing teams.
  • The result: proprietary data turned into shippable AI that dealers can adopt. I'd like to see what's true on the inside.
